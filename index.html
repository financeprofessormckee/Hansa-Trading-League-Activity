    How to build your league
    1. Each city has 100 labor per turn. Invite outsiders and divide production labor between grain and cloth.
    2. The exporter pays transport labor before production. Export only goods the city itself produces.
    3. Preview the council’s verdict. Every newcomer must beat autarky: its best outcome without trade. A member that loses will leave after an executed turn.
    4. Execute six turns. Compare your experiments and explain who benefited, who did not, and why.

    Utility = 2√(grain × cloth), a balanced-consumption index, not money. Equal labor shares maximize autarky utility. Goods are consumed each turn. Costs are deterministic: repeating a plan repeats its outcome.

    Read the economy

    Comparative advantage means giving up less of one good to produce the other. Even a less productive city can have something worth trading.

    Transport consumes labor. A promising exchange can become unprofitable—or impossible—when moving goods takes too much work.

    Bring evidence to the discussion

    1. Who gained most? Did a positive total conceal any individual loss?
    2. Can Cedar contribute despite Alder’s absolute advantage in both goods?
    3. How did the same plan fare when moving goods cost more labor?
    4. Would repeating your best plan teach you anything new?
